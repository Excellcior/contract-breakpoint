Current signals

Strait of Hormuz: LNG transits

Cargo is moving again. Normal operating conditions have not returned.

September recorded 19 LNG cargoes through the strait, the highest monthly total since the war began, according to S&P Global Energy. S&P estimates that if the late-September pace holds into October, full-month transits would recover to about 25% of pre-war levels. The open question is whether the transits can be sustained through winter, and whether escorted convoys continue.

Contract question:If routing, escort arrangements or timing change after signature, which party carries the cost and the delay?

Red Sea / Suez: escorted transit

Traffic is returning faster than protection capacity.

Commercial vessels are effectively queuing for naval escorts, because the EU's Operation Aspides lacks enough warships to meet demand. Carriers are nonetheless restoring Suez capacity, mainly on Asia-Mediterranean services, while the threat near the southern approach widens. Crude transits through Suez last week were the lowest in tonnage terms since mid-July.

Contract question:Does the delivery or laytime assumption in the agreement still hold if the vessel must wait for an escort?
