Current signals

Strait of Hormuz

Flows are recovering. Execution is not yet normal.

Crude and LNG movements through Hormuz have increased materially, but current execution still includes naval protection, reduced-transparency transits and alternative routing arrangements.

The relevant signal is not whether cargo can move. It is whether contractual assumptions built around normal routing, timing and operating conditions still hold.

Red Sea / Suez

Transit is returning under uneven operating conditions.

Suez and Bab el-Mandeb traffic has increased, while security threats remain active and demand for naval escorts has at times exceeded available protection capacity.

Route availability and normal execution are therefore not the same condition.

LNG Delivery Continuity

Physical export recovery does not remove route dependency.

September LNG shipments through Hormuz reached their highest level since the regional conflict began, including renewed Qatar and UAE movements.

The recovery remains dependent on a security environment and execution arrangements that can change independently of the underlying supply commitment.

What these signals show

Execution conditions can change before contractual structures do.

A route can reopen while risk allocation remains abnormal.

Cargo can move while timing assumptions no longer hold.

Operational continuity can return before contractual certainty does.
